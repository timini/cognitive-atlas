# R01 — Cognitive theory and editorial contribution

This is one of five separately commissioned AI assessments of the frozen 100-capital manuscript at `56fa5700fd65d7c0b911af1cce47debdbce62232`, dated 9 September 2026. It is not human peer review, a journal decision or an acceptance prediction. I read the actual source, manuscript inventory, the twelve official-source guides and the saved error-sign-control evidence. I did not read other reviewers' reports or the synthesis. No new analysis, model calls or manuscript rewriting was performed for this review.

## Main scientific assessment

There is something worth pursuing. The study operationalizes a useful distinction between knowing many accurate distance facts and producing a mutually coherent set of relational judgments. Its 100-capital collection also shows that two prompt conditions can have close aggregate accuracy while disagreeing on individual distances and fitted configurations. The strongest observation concerns the **arrangement of errors**, not the raw count of correct answers or an attractive warped coastline. The matched-absolute-error sign control makes that point more defensible by showing why a roughly ten-percent triangle violation rate is not independently surprising at these error magnitudes.

The current empirical contribution is bounded: one Gemini alias, three particular instruction conditions, ten completed slots per pair and one collection period. The paper appropriately acknowledges direct reconstruction precedent. It does not establish distinct language-specific latent maps, human-like cognition, a cultural mechanism or a literal internal representation. It also finds no observed absolute regional benefit for the chosen Arabic and Chinese groups. These limits do not make the data uninteresting. They determine what the result can currently mean.

The larger problem is theoretical underdetermination. Independent retrieval of familiar facts, numerical response heuristics and a shared spatial representation all remain compatible with the measured outputs. The introduction's operational definition of a cognitive map is careful, but the contribution paragraph announces a benchmark and lists empirical extensions. The discussion then describes the findings and their limitations without explaining what cognitive account the findings rule against. The prose has become safer without becoming more explanatory. A cognitive-science paper needs a positive argument, not either anthropomorphic overclaiming or an extended disclaimer.

## Prioritized fixes and new work

1. **State the cognitive question and rival predictions.** Distinguish a descriptive geometry fitted to judgments from the stronger hypothesis that a common relational organization predicts new behavior. Frame geography as a domain with known structure. Explain what evidence would favor a transferable organization over independent task-specific judgments. The current paper has not accomplished this theoretical revision.
2. **Add one diagnostic transfer test.** Fit geometry from numeric estimates, then preregister predictions for independently elicited ordinal proximity judgments on held-out triplets. Compare performance with actual geographic order and explicit simple baselines. Stratify close-distance contrasts to avoid the near-ceiling accuracy of widely separated alternatives. Analyze model disagreements and errors, not merely agreement on obvious facts. Cross-task prediction would support transferable behavioral organization; it would still not uniquely identify an internal mechanism.
3. **Replicate contexts before interpreting language.** Use independently checked translations, more than one phrasing per language, and randomized independent time blocks. Retain English versus localized entity names as an explicit factor if studied. Estimate a language contrast over these conditions instead of treating one translated wording as the language itself. The existing randomized-label tests concern a common response distribution, not equal population median maps; more repetitions do not fix that distinction.
4. **Include additional model families for generalization.** Two additional providers or independently developed model families are more informative than only neighboring Gemini versions. Select them for a declared contrast and budget, and use matched tasks and explicitly documented provider differences. This would establish how widely a pattern recurs. A table of vendor MAEs alone leaves the explanation gap unchanged. Do not start a full paid collection until the question and design are fixed.
5. **Choose whether a human comparison serves a specific claim.** A matched human judgment study or a tested psychological model could establish shared or contrasting distortion patterns. New human participants are not a universal prerequisite for behavioral machine-cognition research. A claim about human-like memory, subjective place experience or psychogeography needs the relevant evidence; numeric API responses do not measure those constructs.
6. **Rewrite the narrative around the inferential chain.** Lead with organization, coherence and transfer; explain each observable before its algorithm; put essential limitations next to the claims they constrain. Keep provenance and large capital lists available as supplements. A main figure should visualize the competing predictions or held-out performance, not merely add another colored coastline.

These are proposed studies and writing actions, not results already obtained. The current data support a careful exploratory paper; readiness for a particular cognition journal depends on its contribution bar and the substantive revision, not on calling the same text a cognitive map paper. Author identities, scientific verification, rights and submission approvals also remain unresolved.
