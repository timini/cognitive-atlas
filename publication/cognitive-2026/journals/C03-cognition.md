## Fit and tone — editorial assessment

The intended cognitive question is relevant, but the current paper does not yet deliver the theoretical advance this venue asks for. “We reconstructed a world from many API calls” remains a methodological description. Its strongest opening is a puzzle about whether spatial knowledge behaves as one transferable representation or as task-conditioned judgments. The present numeric-distance task permits memorized estimates, numerical heuristics and relational computation simultaneously; it cannot adjudicate among them.

A convincing revision would first define rival predictions, then test whether numeric reconstructions predict held-out ordinal, directional or multi-step judgments better than straightforward factual and heuristic baselines. A comparison with a psychological account or suitable human evidence would strengthen the bridge. This is a recommendation, not a claim that every Cognition article needs human experiments. Additional model families could reveal whether the effect generalizes, but identical descriptive measurements across several brands still leave the same explanation gap.

Write a connected theoretical argument, not a tour of metrics and geographic software. Compress audit detail into supporting materials while retaining distinctions between descriptive controls, sampling uncertainty and causal language effects. The current English/Arabic/Chinese instruction differences cannot isolate language; larger numbers of observations do not fix that confound. Honest current verdict: potentially an interesting research program, not yet a strong Cognition submission.
