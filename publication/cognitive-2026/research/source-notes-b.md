Checked 9 September 2026. Direct official Springer journal pages and the publisher policy were readable through the browser tool; no access failure prevented the recorded rules. Search-engine results from aggregators were not used to establish requirements. Guide JSON files enumerate exact checked URLs. No editor was contacted.

The scope/format sections in the guides are paraphrases of verified rules. The contribution, tone, fit and proposed experiments are editorial assessments from reading `paper/main.tex` at `56fa5700fd65d7c0b911af1cce47debdbce62232`; they are neither official decisions nor acceptance predictions.

For CRPI, the article-type page specifies APA 7 whereas the generic guide includes numerical-reference boilerplate. Follow the specific article-type instruction. For Memory & Cognition and Behavior Research Methods, the checked guidelines contain a conditional paragraph about data blinding if a journal uses double-blind review. That does not verify either journal's actual review model. Their upload file types, numerical body limits and current OA prices remain unverified rather than guessed. PB&R explicitly specifies double-anonymous review.

The current [Springer AI policy](https://link.springer.com/brands/springer/journal-policies) distinguishes assistive, interpretive and replacement uses. It requires transparent human responsibility; it also prohibits delegating journal peer review to an LLM. These public, author-commissioned preparation reports must not be passed off as journal-appointed independent peer review. No claim that assistant-produced material is automatically submission-compliant is made.

None of the checked pages establishes a universal requirement to collect human-participant data for every computational article. Human-cognition relevance is a genuine scope consideration for PB&R and Memory & Cognition; a human comparator is an editorial recommendation where it would make the inference stronger.
