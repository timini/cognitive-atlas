Dear Editors,

Please consider “Accurate Distances, Imperfect Geometry: Repeated Language-Model Judgments across 100 Capitals” as an original research article for Transactions in GIS.

The study asks what geographic configuration is implied by repeated numerical distance judgments from a language model. Across 100 capitals and three fixed instruction conditions, it connects factual accuracy, metric consistency and spherical reconstruction. Its contribution is an empirical benchmark and controlled interpretation of those properties: the work does not introduce a new embedding algorithm or claim direct access to a neural representation.

An additional control preserves every pair’s observed absolute error while randomizing error signs. It shows why a raw triangle-violation percentage should not, by itself, be interpreted as unusually incoherent spatial knowledge. The paper also distinguishes fitted capital positions from coastline interpolation and conditional response-distribution comparisons from general language effects. Regional conclusions are descriptive; the appendix retains the bootstrap calibration failure that limits inferential use of those comparisons.

The subject fits the journal’s interest in geographic representation, spatial analysis and uncertainty. Observations, exact prompts, geographic references, analysis code and computed diagnostics are publicly available. The manuscript describes substantial assistance from OpenAI Codex in code, analysis, translation, figures and drafting, as distinct from Gemini 3.5 Flash as the measured system.

The paper has been posted publicly at https://timini.github.io/cognitive-atlas/paper/ and its source at https://github.com/timini/cognitive-atlas. That history is disclosed for the editor’s assessment under the journal’s prior-publication policy.
