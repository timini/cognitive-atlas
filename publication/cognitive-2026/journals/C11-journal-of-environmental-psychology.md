## Verified scope and article routes

The [official Elsevier description](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-environmental-psychology/0272-4944) centers psychological processes in reciprocal relations between individuals and physical environments. It welcomes empirical single- and multi-study articles, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, brief empirical notes, replications, registered reports and letters. The theoretical, methodological or practical contribution should concern human–environment relations.

## Current AI policy

The [Elsevier policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals) allows supportive AI assistance with literature, organization and readability under human oversight. Authors must verify output, retain their own intellectual contribution, disclose tool/purpose/oversight in a manuscript declaration, and describe research-process AI in Methods. AI cannot be an author. Producing sections without genuine author intellectual contribution is inappropriate. Actual human responsibility and rights review are therefore still needed; disclosure alone is not automatic eligibility.

## Editorial tone and scientific bar — assessment, not publisher rules

This is a future target rather than a first submission choice for the current study. To justify it, explain how language-mediated representations of environments bear on a psychological process. A model can be a comparative system, a proposed account of language-derived spatial knowledge, or an experimental tool, but a vendor's distance accuracy is not itself a result about human–environment relations.

The user's psychogeographic ambition is compatible with asking how places become subjectively arranged, but this dataset does not measure lived experience, affect, walking practices, place attachment or human environmental perception. Calling warped coastlines psychogeography would not supply those missing constructs. Conversely, the lack of human participants is not being asserted as a formal journal prohibition: theoretical or computational work can motivate a psychological contribution if that bridge is substantiated.

A stronger extension would compare model and independently collected human distance/relative-proximity judgments, or test whether a language-derived account predicts a documented cognitive distortion in held-out settings. If no human study is feasible, make a specific computational account and test discriminating predictions rather than attaching speculative cultural explanations. Additional model families alone would improve AI generality but would not establish this journal's human–environment relevance.
