## Cognitive contribution and tone — editorial assessment

**Possible methodological route, currently incomplete.** This would be a paper about how to measure elicited relational representations reliably. Its audience needs to know when repeated judgments plus reconstruction recover useful structure, what biases the measurement introduces, and how another cognitive scientist can use the procedure. CSV storage and a polished atlas are implementation decisions, not the methodological contribution.

The present error-preserving controls, ellipsoid/sphere comparison, restart checks, and bootstrap failure audit are promising foundations. The failure audit is particularly relevant because it shows that apparently ample repeated observations can still give unreliable inference. But a single rare-tail scenario does not validate a general uncertainty procedure, and assembling established methods does not automatically constitute a new method.

A stronger manuscript would compare alternative estimators and sampling designs under prespecified simulation scenarios, report recovery and calibration rather than only convergence, demonstrate the method on real observations from more than one system, and include a concise worked example. Synthetic validation data must remain explicitly distinct from recorded model judgments. A second representational domain might demonstrate general utility, but is not mandatory if the geographic validation is sufficiently diagnostic.

Write a measurement argument with clear assumptions, decision rules, failure cases and reusable instructions. Explain why the procedure improves cognitive research over simply calculating error rates or applying off-the-shelf MDS. The current benchmark narrative and untested generalization leave that contribution underdeveloped. More models are useful as validation cases; they cannot substitute for method validation.
