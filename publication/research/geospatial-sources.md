# Geospatial journal source audit

## Shared publisher policies

### Taylor & Francis: verified policy; material eligibility concern

The [AI guidance](https://authorservices.taylorandfrancis.com/editorial-policies/using-ai-in-your-research-and-manuscript-preparations/) states: “Taylor & Francis does not allow generative AI to create a first draft of a manuscript or sections of a manuscript.” Its permitted cases include copyediting, checked translation, documented AI research methodologies, and research-data visualizations; the latter require disclosure in legends. These exceptions do not expressly authorize AI-written first drafts. The page also requires authors to identify the tool/version and confirm accuracy, responsibility, and suitable terms. Its statements about research methods allow this project's measurement of LLM responses; that does not resolve the separate manuscript-authorship issue.

The [overarching AI policy](https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/ai-policy/) requires disclosure and human accountability, forbids AI authorship, and warns against replacing core researcher responsibilities. The policy permits some research visualizations, so an assertion that all AI-assisted figures are prohibited would be incorrect. It reserves editorial discretion. **Our assessment:** substantial assistant drafting in this project's history creates an eligibility hold for J01/J04/J05. Honest disclosure and direct editorial clarification are necessary; neither a cosmetic rewrite nor adding a statement is established as a cure. Do not claim that the manuscript was merely copyedited.

T&F's [formatting resource](https://authorservices.taylorandfrancis.com/publishing-your-research/writing-your-paper/formatting-and-templates/) makes LaTeX acceptance and format-free eligibility journal-specific. Its [data-policy directory](https://authorservices.taylorandfrancis.com/data-sharing-policies/) similarly requires checking the particular journal's tier. We have therefore not assigned a permissive template or “basic” sharing tier to journals whose instructions could not be inspected. The [submission resource](https://authorservices.taylorandfrancis.com/publishing-your-research/making-your-submission/) is a preparation starting point, not evidence of a particular journal's mandatory cover-letter fields.

### Wiley: verified policy incorporated by Transactions in GIS

The journal's instructions explicitly link Wiley ethics guidance. The current [research-integrity and publishing-ethics policy](https://authors.wiley.com/ethics-guidelines/index.html) requires human responsibility, substantive AI-use disclosure, and verification of generated material. AI cannot be an author. Provider terms must protect publication/content rights, including restrictions concerning training on author content. Editors decide permissibility. Routine spelling/grammar assistance is treated differently from substantive development; this project's assistance falls in the latter category. The policy requires adequate methods reporting and a data-availability statement. **Our recommendation:** retain a complete human-verified account of AI roles, separate generation of experimental observations from assistance with writing/code, and check the actual tool/account terms. Do not infer consent or rights compliance from an API key or a publicly available repository. The five requested AI readiness reports are not substitutes for human responsibility or journal peer review.

### Elsevier: verified publisher policy for CEUS

The [generative-AI policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/generative-ai-policies-for-journals) distinguishes manuscript-preparation disclosure from research-method reporting. Authors must identify substantive manuscript assistance and its purpose/oversight in an AI declaration; research uses belong in Methods. Humans remain responsible and AI cannot be an author. Basic proofreading is exempt, but substantive restructuring is not. **Our recommendation:** document the actual assistance throughout development, not just Gemini as the experimental subject. The [publishing-ethics policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/publishing-ethics) governs author responsibilities and originality. The [author policy overview](https://www-prod.elsevier.com/researcher/author/policies-and-guidelines) identifies electronic preprints as an exception to prior publication; disclose the existing public PDF rather than assuming all repository histories are journal publications. Journal-specific package rules remain unverified where its guide was blocked.

## Ranking within this six-journal subset: our judgment

1. **Transactions in GIS:** strongest practical first target; geographic uncertainty, computation and reproducible spatial analysis are central to the work. Human authorship/disclosures and a shorter abstract still need work.
2. **Spatial Cognition & Computation:** excellent intellectual match, but the first-draft AI policy creates a hold and theoretical cognitive interpretation needs care.
3. **Cartography and Geographic Information Science:** good match if the paper foregrounds measured geometry and the limits of deformation; same AI-policy hold.
4. **International Journal of Geographical Information Science:** strong topical but demanding contribution fit; AI provenance and public-identity rules create two eligibility holds.
5. **Computers, Environment and Urban Systems:** conditional fallback; global capital distances do not yet establish an urban/environmental systems application.
6. **Applied Spatial Analysis and Policy:** weak current fit; no tested policy question or decision consequence. A genuinely applied extension would be preferable to inserting speculative policy rhetoric.

These rankings are neither acceptance probabilities nor impact-factor rankings. The guides deliberately distinguish verified rules, recommendations, and inaccessible details.
