## Preparation priorities for this manuscript

This is a research direction candidate rather than a recommended immediate submission. Distances between national capitals assess one form of spatial knowledge; they do not show that a model can make reliable urban planning decisions. A persuasive application would need an explicit decision task, a relevant error tolerance and evaluation of how incorrect distances affect outcomes. Those measurements are absent from the current paper and should not be invented in a rewritten introduction.

If the authors want to retain the current study unchanged in scope, prioritize a geographic-information or interdisciplinary methods venue. If an applied extension is later collected, it should complement this fixed 100-capital benchmark with clearly identified new evidence. The existing study’s uncertainty and consistency measures could motivate such an extension but are not themselves its result.

For writing, keep the language conditions and population boundaries visible, explain why geodesic distance is the target, and distinguish sphere-fitting error from coastline interpolation artifacts. Do not call pairwise judgments route estimates or substitute route distances to match an urban framing.

The formatting side remains deliberately provisional. The official ScienceDirect guide and an Elsevier alternative both returned 403, including a direct HTTP fallback. An accessible official Elsevier Shop page establishes scope, but does not establish abstract limits, highlights requirements, review anonymity or LaTeX instructions. Obtain those fields from the live guide or editorial office before creating a compliant package. Third-party template sites and a similarly named ScienceDirect-lookalike domain were excluded from the evidence base.
