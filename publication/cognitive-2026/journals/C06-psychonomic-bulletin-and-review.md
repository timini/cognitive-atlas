## Cognitive contribution and tone — editorial assessment

**An ambitious later target, not a sensible submission of the present manuscript.** The argument must produce a substantial theoretical advance relevant to a broad cognitive audience. Showing that Gemini gives accurate but imperfect distances across 100 capitals is an observation; the bridge to cognition would be a theoretically diagnostic pattern, such as coherent relational inference versus isolated distance retrieval, or a shared distortion signature across human judgments and language systems.

The current sign-control comparison helps: it prevents interpreting every metric violation as a special representational failure. It does not distinguish a cognitive mechanism. The translated-prompt differences are also insufficient to claim different cognitive worlds, because wording and language changed together and distributional differences need not imply different median maps.

A suitable brief report would organize one clear hypothesis, one decisive test and a small number of conceptually legible figures. Move the reproducibility machinery and numerical audit to supplementary materials. Replace benchmark-oriented motivation with a theoretical puzzle, and conclude with what the result constrains about a cognitive account. Do not merely substitute “cognitive” for “geographic” throughout.

Additional models would support breadth, but a model leaderboard would still fall short. A compact factorial study with preregistered contrasts, reviewed translations and a task that tests transfer of the inferred relations is more persuasive. A human comparison could materially strengthen relevance to this journal; this is a fit recommendation, not a formal rule that every submission must recruit people.
