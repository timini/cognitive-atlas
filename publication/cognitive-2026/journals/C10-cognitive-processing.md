## Editorial tone and scientific bar — assessment, not publisher rules

This is the strongest practical intellectual match among C09–C12: its explicit inclusion of artificial cognition accommodates model behavior without pretending to have tested human participants. However, the present manuscript's contribution paragraph reads as an empirical geospatial extension. Recast the introduction around acquisition of spatial relations through language, coherence of elicited knowledge, and sensitivity to context. Theories should yield distinct observable predictions, not merely supply cognitive-map vocabulary.

A useful progression is: what the model gets right about ordering; what inconsistency survives matched-error controls; which inferred relationships transfer to a different judgment task. Explain why the final quantity bears on representation. The error-sign control prevents overinterpreting a visually striking map, while the current language/wording confound limits contextual explanations. Do not promote statistical rejection of response-distribution equality into evidence of distinct latent maps.

A new model-family comparison would improve generality. Pair it with independently checked translations, prompt paraphrases and separate collection sessions. Cross-task prediction or a theoretically specified bias test would add more conceptual value than a larger table of MAEs. Human data are useful if claiming human-like biases; no universal human-participant requirement appears in the inspected scope.
