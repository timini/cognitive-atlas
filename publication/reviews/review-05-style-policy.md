## Prioritized writing and policy findings

1. **Complete the human scientific and disclosure record before formatting.** P21 has an empty author field and P220 says identities remain unassigned. Real contributors must establish authorship, affiliations where applicable, correspondence, contributions, funding/conflicts and responsibility. A review agent cannot supply those attestations. P47 describes Gemini as the experimental subject and P49 acknowledges translations; P220 mentions ten AI-assisted reviews. None is a complete account of substantive assistant support for drafting, analysis and code. Describe actual roles and checks without relabeling them as proofreading. Journal-specific locations differ: ACL acknowledgments, several journals’ Methods, Elsevier’s additional pre-reference declaration, and TMLR’s first-page footnote.

2. **Treat restrictive AI rules as eligibility questions, not style tasks.** J01/J04/J05 and the PeerJ-linked T&F policy raise an explicit first-draft conflict; IJGIS also raises online-identity concerns. Royal Society Open Science restricts scientific writing assistance more tightly than this history appears to satisfy. AI & SOCIETY strongly discourages broader use; that is not identical to a categorical ban. TMLR’s FAQ and general policy need reconciliation for extensive assistance. Data Science Journal’s older broad data-generation wording needs a ruling on measured LLM outputs. Do not hide the project’s history, assume a rewrite cures it, or describe genuine measured responses as fabricated data. These are holds or clarification needs, not journal rejections already received.

3. **Make the abstract select the result rather than enumerate the analysis.** P26 has a strong first sentence, but then stacks three MAEs, a correlation threshold, triangle rates, fitted displacement, a control displacement, pair disagreement, map disagreement, randomization, local groups and sensitivity. A specialist can decode it; a general reader has little time to understand why each measure matters. Keep the sample/model/conditions, the accuracy-versus-consistency contrast, one spatial effect size and the language/wording boundary. Cut secondary regional and quality-control detail before cutting the key null caveat. Transactions in GIS requires a real cut to 150 words; Scientific Reports/RSOS need a careful final count near 200. The 194-token inventory is not proof of every publisher’s counting convention.

4. **Remove revision-process prose from the final scientific voice.** The dated subtitle at P22, “This revision independently checks” at P81, “the only experimental inputs to this revised manuscript” in the appendix, and the extended lock-development narrative at P214–218 read partly as release notes. A journal reader needs the final design, evidence and limitations. Describe the twelve-start diagnostic as a study analysis; move implementation-history detail into provenance documentation while retaining the inability to reconstruct historical dirty-source state. Keep the current 100-capital scope throughout. The strong operational definition at P36 and explicit antecedents at P32 should remain.

5. **Match visual emphasis to inferential strength.** P94–98 carefully states a response-distribution null; P202–208 distinguishes observations from general language effects. That restraint is a strength. However, the main regional table still gives small p-values visual authority, while P263 reports that an admissible rare-tail diagnostic can collapse interval coverage. I recommend descriptive regional gains, group sizes, distance balancing and omission sensitivities in the main narrative; place fragile conditional probabilities and calibration in a clearly labeled audit/supplement. This is an editorial recommendation, not a new test result or proof that the actual response distribution has rare tails. Independent prompt/date replication is needed for broader claims; it is not automatically required to publish a carefully bounded descriptive study.

6. **Preserve the meaning of the maps through conversion.** P83–87 and the map caption correctly separate global alignment, display projection and illustrative coastline interpolation. Maintain that explanation at final figure size. Use “fitted capital displacement” where “map distortion” could mean a measured coastline property. Scientific Reports has ten current main display items against eight allowed: move or combine at least two, not all technical controls. PLOS’s LaTeX instructions require special attention despite general format-free wording. PeerJ and EPJ have individual-file limits below the current ZIP size. A template conversion must not quietly drop essential null/denominator/calibration information into unreviewed appendices.

7. **Create a citable, licensed release; do not confuse this with access repair.** P214 needs its privacy sentence corrected. DOI archiving is explicitly required for Data Science Journal and PeerJ’s social-platform software route; other guides vary between requirements and recommendations. The absent reuse-license record remains separate from downloadability. Humans with relevant rights must choose and approve licenses; a reviewer cannot invent permissions. Likewise, no known fee quote establishes institutional coverage or an author’s consent to pay.
