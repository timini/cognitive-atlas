## Scientific value and actual tone

There is enough here to justify a serious next study, but not a major explanatory discovery about cognition. The reusable observation is that accurate individual relations, global metric consistency and response stability are different properties. Pair-median MAEs of 216–234 km coexist with rank correlations above 0.998 and 10.7–13.3% triangle violations. A fitted sphere offers an interpretable summary, but its coherence is imposed by the fitting model. Its input-to-fit neighborhood decline, from about 96% to 92–93%, usefully exposes a cost of enforcing that coherence. These are measurements of expressed judgments, not evidence of an internal spatial store. [M: Abstract; Analysis; Results. GE: geometry diagnostics.]

The descriptive error-sign control is scientifically valuable because it reduces the temptation to oversell inconsistency. It preserves each pair’s absolute error and produces broadly similar violation rates: about 11.5–12.3%. English and Chinese lie below their respective control ranges and Arabic above, which describes signed error arrangement under this artificial construction. The ranges are not confidence intervals, the signs are not a validated inferential null, and none of this identifies retrieval, relational computation or a cultural mechanism. This modest but important correction is more useful than a dramatic warped-map claim. [M: An absolute-error-preserving control; High rank accuracy coexists with metric inconsistency. EC: conditions and summary.]

The approximately 79–88 km between-condition shifts are observed fitted differences. Conditional permutations concern equality of within-pair response distributions under assumed exchangeability, not equality of population median maps; dispersion differences can contribute. English, Arabic and Chinese prompts also differ in explanatory wording, so the design cannot isolate language. The regional groups show no observed absolute advantage, with gains of about −8.9 and −10.7 km; they are descriptive after the bootstrap calibration failure. Neither nonsignificance nor these negative point estimates proves a general absence of benefit. [M: Conditional comparisons; regional Results; Discussion. CA: rare_extreme_skew.]

The current prose is admirably restrained but substantially geographic and methodological. The title promises accurate distances and imperfect geometry; the opening question is what configuration best reconciles the answers. The fourth introduction paragraph explicitly calls the contribution a global repeated-judgment benchmark and an empirical extension of established methods. The fifth defines a cognitive map operationally and correctly says memorized facts, heuristics and internal computation all remain compatible. Those are clear boundaries, but not rival hypotheses with different predictions. The Discussion explains estimands and cautions well; it does not show which cognitive account lost plausibility. The abstract’s closing benchmark claim and running title reinforce that orientation. A positive theory-led argument must be added; replacing geographic vocabulary with cognitive vocabulary would not supply it. [M: title; Abstract; Introduction paragraphs 1, 4–6; Discussion; Conclusion.]

The empirical record is substantial: 148,500 completed slots, 148,504 attempts and 148,491 valid values. Yet the relevant generalization units are not 148,500 independent cognitive experiments. This is one alias, date and unvalidated wording per condition with clustered requests. Historical collection used unconstrained text and a strict whole-response parser; later schema collectors are excluded. Parser replay and stable solver restarts address implementation, not stationarity, native-speaker fidelity, hidden mechanisms or response-tail coverage. Public materials support inspection, but missing historical execution records, permanent archiving, reuse licenses and actual human authorship facts remain material. [M: Data and elicitation; Data availability and research provenance. QC; GE; I.]

## Experiments ranked by cognitive information value

These are proposed studies, not work completed or a request to start API collection. No new empirical result or external theoretical claim is asserted here.

1. **Make geometry predict a different, held-out judgment.** Prespecify rival behavioral models: a single fitted relational geometry, direct numerical-distance lookup, a simple reference-geography estimator, and an explicitly defined categorical or distance-based heuristic. Fit on a declared subset of numeric edges and evaluate independently elicited ordinal proximity/triplet judgments, with enough training edges to identify the intended geometry. Most ordinary triplets may be trivial because geographic rank accuracy is already high; choose diagnostic items where the accounts predict different answers, report an unselected test set too, and keep test responses out of item selection and tuning. Test whether geometry predicts the model’s errors beyond raw numeric estimates and true distance, not merely whether it recovers Earth. Report pair/capital dependence and compare predictive loss with calibrated uncertainty. This could show transferable behavioral organization; even success would not prove a hidden map, exclude every retrieval model, or establish human-like cognition. Failure would directly limit the operational map’s usefulness. [Motivated by M: Recovering and aligning geometry; Introduction; Discussion.]

2. **Separate a stable prompt-condition pattern from one wording and collection episode.** Obtain independent fluent review of translations, use multiple meaning-matched paraphrases per language, and randomize conditions/pairs within independently repeated time blocks. Hold name localization as a separate factor. Retain all terminal outputs and format failures. Prespecify which effects concern central estimates, variability, signed residual structure and transferred judgments, and which sampling population each estimate covers. Stronger replication of a specified prompt-language effect is possible; arbitrary differences between languages’ texts still do not create a pure language intervention or a cultural mechanism. Use a stratified pilot to choose repeats/block counts by precision and calibration rather than by the previous number ten. This design work is a prerequisite to interpreting the transfer experiment, though transfer has the highest conceptual payoff. [M: Three matched prompt conditions; Discussion.]

3. **Challenge factual retrieval with controlled novel relations.** In a separate study, supply unfamiliar place names and a known artificial relational structure, manipulate an edge or category cue, and test previously unprovided relations in another task. Prespecify competing predictions and counterbalance name and description choices. Systematic transfer after a controlled change would support flexible use of supplied relations and constrain an account based solely on memorized real-world capital distances. It would still not identify architecture or subjective spatial experience, and it would concern in-context relational learning rather than retrospectively explain the current real-capital dataset. This is more cognitively diagnostic than expanding the capital list. [M: Introduction’s compatible explanations and behavioral-planning distinction.]

4. **Add model families to the informative design, not as a standalone leaderboard.** Yes, add a small number of independently developed families after stabilizing the protocol, ideally including an inspectable model if intervention studies are genuinely planned. A manageable initial comparison is the present family plus two distinct families, selected for a prespecified contrast and documented snapshots rather than assumed provider independence. First run the same controlled subset, prompts, tasks and time blocks across all included models; a new model compared only with the old Gemini run confounds family with date and collection procedure. Compare residual signatures and transfer, not only MAE. Report actual API settings and output protocols because similarly named settings are not a guarantee of equivalent sampling. Replication would broaden the phenomenon beyond one selected alias; heterogeneity would bound it. Neither result alone identifies training-data causes, architecture effects, human cognition or culture, and a few chosen families do not represent all LLMs. [M: Data and elicitation; Discussion.]

5. **Validate the uncertainty procedure before making stronger population-map claims.** Extend simulation and recovery tests across ties, rounding, unequal dispersion, rare tails, clustered dependence and known geometric alternatives, keeping synthetic records visibly separate from model outputs. Compare estimators and sampling designs against declared targets. Existing bootstrap failures motivate this work but are not proof of actual rare tails; more permutations cannot fix missing modes or dependence. If uncertainty remains poorly calibrated, keep claims descriptive and invest in sampling design rather than cosmetic probability precision. This becomes priority one for a Behavior Research Methods paper, although it supplies measurement credibility more directly than cognitive explanation. [M: Empirical-bootstrap audit and calibration. CA.]

6. **Add human evidence when the intended claim requires a human bridge.** For Memory & Cognition, Environmental Psychology or the Frontiers Cognition section, a carefully matched human judgment task or a validated, explicitly specified cognitive account can be high value. Match entity familiarity, task meaning and scoring; compare residual patterns after accounting for reference distance and rounding, not merely two high accuracy correlations. Human similarity would support a comparative behavioral claim, not shared implementation. A theory tested against suitable existing human evidence may be enough for a narrower computational argument; new participants are not a universal journal rule. Participant work, if chosen, needs its own prospective design and appropriate ethics. A study of experience, affect or place attachment is necessary only if those constructs become the research question; the current distances cannot measure them by renaming. [G07, G11, G12: scope and fit; M: Study materials.]

The economical decision is a smaller, well-designed discriminating study before another complete 100-capital sweep. If the only feasible extension is more provider matrices, the result becomes a stronger comparative behavioral benchmark, which has value, but my high-theory venue verdicts largely stay unchanged. If the aim is mechanism, an inspectable model helps only when paired with an actual intervention design. If the aim is human cognition, provider diversity is an inadequate substitute for the relevant psychological comparison. Preserve the current corrected paper as exploratory evidence; preregister only new work.

## Twelve venue-specific verdicts

- **C01 — Cognitive Science:** Strong intellectual aspiration, weak immediate submission. Develop a falsifiable account of relational judgments and demonstrate transfer; adding families alone does not meet the multidisciplinary contribution bar.
- **C02 — Topics in Cognitive Science:** Conditional collection route only. These data could be one case in an integrative topic, but no standalone research submission is presently available and a broader model panel does not change eligibility.
- **C03 — Cognition:** Do not submit the frozen paper. A held-out test that discriminates cognitive accounts is the critical advance; multiple vendors repeating the same description would still fall short.
- **C04 — Cognitive Psychology:** Retain as a later theory-program target. Explicit model comparison and converging evidence are needed; neither a longer paper nor more brands supplies integrative explanation.
- **C05 — Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications:** Plausible after a substantive practical-cognition framing and a planned second study. Demonstrate why cross-question consistency matters for use of spatial information, then replicate with another family.
- **C06 — Psychonomic Bulletin & Review:** Not a persuasive Brief Report yet. A compact, decisive cognitive result would matter more than the current large descriptive dataset; a human or established cognitive-model comparison could strengthen broad relevance.
- **C07 — Memory & Cognition:** Conditional on a developed retrieval/reconstruction argument. Matched human judgments or a prediction-bearing cognitive model would add more fit than vendor breadth alone.
- **C08 — Behavior Research Methods:** Credible alternative research direction if measurement validation becomes the contribution. Prioritize estimator recovery, uncertainty calibration and sampling-design comparisons; extra models serve as demonstration cases.
- **C09 — Spatial Cognition & Computation:** Best direct spatial subject fit, but AI-drafting policy is a material eligibility hold. Even if clarified, add cross-task prediction and reliable elicitation replication; do not assume a human rewrite erases provenance.
- **C10 — Cognitive Processing:** Best practical cognitive destination in this assessment after substantive revision and focused new evidence. Artificial cognition is explicit in scope; test transfer and repeat elicitation rather than aiming for a leaderboard.
- **C11 — Journal of Environmental Psychology:** Poor current fit. A genuine human–environment or validated psychological-model bridge is needed; extra model families do not measure environmental experience or establish that bridge.
- **C12 — Frontiers in Psychology — Cognition:** Conditional on satisfying the section’s psychological-theory foundation. The larger article route is available, but a label change and more model rows would not create the missing cognitive-process contribution.
