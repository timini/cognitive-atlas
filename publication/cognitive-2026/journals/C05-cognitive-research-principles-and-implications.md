## Cognitive contribution and tone — editorial assessment

**Plausible after substantial reframing and a stronger second study.** Explain the practical problem that motivates the cognitive question: people use language systems as sources of spatial knowledge, but success on isolated questions may conceal incompatible relational judgments. The intellectual target is the relationship between factual recall, relational coherence and context-sensitive elicitation. The coastline visualization can make that distinction intelligible, but is not the theoretical result.

The current manuscript has useful evidence about within-condition consistency, response variation and changes under prompt conditions. Its introduction mostly positions a geographical benchmark. It does not yet explain what a cognitive account predicts differently from independent retrieval of facts or a simple geographic heuristic. A Significance Statement that merely claims AI maps are useful would not fix this.

Use accessible cognitive terms and define the observable quantities before naming algorithms. Present MDS and the sphere as measurement instruments. Keep the limitations, but integrate each with the inference it constrains instead of allowing a catalogue of caveats to replace the positive finding. An additional model is valuable if it tests whether the same distortion pattern survives a different system; a second prompt formulation and independent collection block are also needed to distinguish representational regularity from a single elicitation protocol.
