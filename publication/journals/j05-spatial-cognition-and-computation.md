## Preparation priorities for this manuscript

The central writing challenge is conceptual precision. A stable distance judgment is not necessarily a stable cognitive representation; a sphere that fits responses is not evidence that the model stores a sphere. The current manuscript correctly treats its geometry as an operational description. Preserve that framing in the title, abstract and discussion, even when a more anthropomorphic phrase would be more memorable.

The strongest cognitive contribution is the dissociation between fine distance ranking and inconsistent metric structure. Explain why that distinction matters for models of spatial knowledge, and which additional tasks would discriminate memorized pair facts, linguistic heuristics and transferable spatial reasoning. Those tasks remain proposals until measured.

The crosslinguistic result is especially relevant but also especially vulnerable. The experiment changes natural-language instructions while keeping canonical English entity names, and translations differ in their explanatory content. Native-speaker validation and multiple phrasings would strengthen a new study; the existing data cannot isolate language from wording. A reviewer should not need to find that caveat only in the appendix.

Before a submission package is made, resolve the publisher’s AI first-draft restriction in light of the actual development history. This policy question does not disappear because AI is the experimental subject. Then check the blocked numerical formatting rules and the exact author-anonymity mode. The official overview says referees are anonymous, which is not by itself evidence that author names must be hidden.
